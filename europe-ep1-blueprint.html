Europe Trip Planning - Package Tour vs Self Planned Tour - Ep 1

Written during our Europe trip planning phase in March 2023. Prices and rules may have changed since, so please check before you book.

If you are planning to take a trip to Europe anytime in the future, then you have come to the right place. I have prepared a complete series of guides that will cover all stages of a Europe trip—right from planning to tickets, Schengen Visa, actual travel, where to go, where to stay, and what to eat.

In today's episode, which is Episode 1, we will focus on the biggest question: How should you travel to Europe? Should you take a package tour with operators like SOTC, Thomas Cook, or MakeMyTrip, or should you travel and plan everything on your own?

We will do a detailed comparison of the pros and cons of both options across six key factors. At the end, I will tell you which option I selected for my family's Europe trip!

1. Meals and Food Options

Package tour operators usually have breakfast and dinner included, and they often tie up with restaurants where you can eat an Indian buffet. If you love eating out every day, a package tour is great.

However, for a pure vegetarian Indian family, eating restaurant-style food continuously for 14 days can be a challenge. We personally cannot digest outside food for more than 2-3 days and prefer homemade meals. When planning your own travel, you have the flexibility to book an Airbnb or apartment hotel, visit local grocery stores, and cook your own meals, or simply heat up ready-to-eat packets brought from India.

Winner: Self-Planned Tour (1 Point)

2. Convenience and Luggage

In a package tour, you are provided with a dedicated bus right from the airport. This vehicle stays with you for the entire duration of your trip. You just dump your luggage in the bus and relax. You don't have to worry about carrying heavy suitcases from one place to another.

In Europe, you have to walk a lot, and cabs are incredibly expensive. If you plan your own trip, you will likely rely on trains and buses. Commuting with heavy luggage and kids between train stations, bus stops, and hotels can be physically exhausting.

Winner: Package Tour (1 Point)

3. Itinerary Pace

Tour packages have a rigidly fixed itinerary. The operator's goal is to tick off 5 to 7 countries in just 14 days, resulting in a very hectic schedule. You will likely start your day at 9 AM, spend the entire day running from one spot to another in a bus, and return to your hotel exhausted by 7:30 PM, only to repeat it the next day.

If you are on a family holiday, you do not want to be running a 9-to-6 shift. A self-planned tour allows you to control the pace. If you like a city, you can spend an extra day. If you want to skip a museum and just laze around in your hotel room, you have the freedom to do so.

Winner: Self-Planned Tour (1 Point)

4. Personalization and Experiences

A package tour does not account for personal preferences. Some people want to see historical monuments and cathedrals, some want to experience European nightlife, while others prefer natural landscapes or shopping.

When you travel on your own, the world is yours to design. For instance, I personally wanted to experience driving a Tesla and a Ferrari in Europe—something absolutely impossible on a rigid package tour schedule. By planning my own itinerary, I reserved an entire day just to enjoy driving these cars.

Winner: Self-Planned Tour (1 Point)

5. Ticket Bookings and Logistics

With a package tour, you pay the money and buy peace of mind. The operator handles everything: flights, Schengen Visa, intercity travel, local transport, and entry tickets to museums and attractions.

When planning your own trip, you have to manage everything from scratch. You have to navigate the visa process, figure out local city bus passes, book Eurail tickets, and stand in lines for tourist attractions. This can be overwhelming and challenging for many people.

Winner: Package Tour (1 Point)

6. Overall Cost

If you check standard 14-day Europe packages (including lunch and dinner), they average around ₹2.5 to ₹3 Lakhs per person.

When you travel on your own, you can significantly optimize this cost. You can redeem credit card reward points to get free or heavily discounted flights. You save a lot of money by preparing your own dinners. You can buy cost-effective Eurail passes for intercity travel, and use credit card discounts to book local attractions online.

Winner: Self-Planned Tour (1 Point)

The Final Verdict

Based on the scoring above, the Self-Planned Tour wins with 4 points, while the Package Tour scores 2 points.

Even if it requires a bit more effort upfront, I chose to plan my family's trip on my own. It allowed us to comfortably visit 3 to 4 countries at our own pace, cook our own vegetarian meals, and enjoy personalized experiences.

Everyone's travel preferences are different, so use these 6 factors to calculate what works best for your family. In the next episode, we will cover how to select the right time to travel and how to shortlist your cities!
