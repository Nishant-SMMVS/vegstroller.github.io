Europe Trip Planning - E2 - How To Plan A Perfect Europe Trip

Written during our Europe trip planning phase in March 2023. Prices and seasons remain generally consistent, but always check current guidelines before you book.

Planning a trip to Europe can seem overwhelming. With so many countries, cultures, and landscapes clustered together, it's easy to get confused about where to start, when to go, and how to manage the budget.

In Episode 2 of our Europe Trip Planning series (following up on our Package Tour vs. Self-Planned debate), we are assuming you have decided to travel on your own. Now, we will focus on how to actually get started with planning your perfect Europe trip.

1. Choose the Best Time to Visit Europe

The best time to visit Europe depends entirely on the type of experience you want:

  • Winter (December to March): It will be very cold, but you can enjoy Christmas markets, New Year celebrations, and Halloween. However, daylight is very short—it can get dark as early as 3:30 PM or 4:00 PM, which limits your sightseeing time. It's also the only time to see the Northern Lights.
  • Summer (June to August): This is the peak tourist season. The sun shines brightly from 5:30 AM until almost 9:00 PM, giving you endless hours to explore. Keep in mind that everything will be crowded and much more expensive.
  • Spring (March to May) & Autumn (September to November): These shoulder seasons are often the sweet spot. The weather is pleasant, and you get to see Europe either blooming with fresh greenery (Spring) or beautiful fall colors (Autumn). If your goal is to see the tulips in the Netherlands, Spring is mandatory.

2. Determine the Duration of Your Trip

For most Indian families, Europe is a once-in-a-lifetime opportunity due to the expenses involved. Because of this, some people try to cram 7 countries into a single week. Do not do this.

The minimum recommended duration for a Europe trip is 7 to 10 days, but ideally, you want 2 to 4 weeks. Your goal should be to enjoy a relaxing holiday, not a stressful 9-to-6 job where you are just running from one train station to another.

3. How to Decide What to Visit

This is where most people get stuck. They want to see hundreds of things but have limited time. To solve this, use my 4-step formula:

  1. Take a pen and paper and write down every place you have ever dreamed of visiting in Europe.
  2. Rank them in order of your absolute top preferences.
  3. Group those destinations by country.
  4. Identify the Top 3 Countries you want to visit. Even on a leisurely 2-week trip, 2 to 3 countries is the maximum you should attempt to cover.

Once you have your top 3 countries, stop overthinking it. To book your flights, you only need your entry and exit destinations. Because the Schengen Visa allows seamless travel across borders, you can map out the detailed day-to-day inner itinerary later.

4. Maintain Flexibility in Your Itinerary

Try to keep your dates flexible. Zero in on the approximate week you want to depart and return, and keep a buffer of plus or minus 2-3 days. This flexibility will help you secure much cheaper flight tickets.

5. Determine Your Budget

As discussed in Episode 1, a realistic budget for a self-planned, comfortable trip to Europe (spanning roughly two weeks) should hover around ₹2,80,000 to ₹3,17,000 per person. You can bring this down significantly by cooking your own meals, using public transport, and utilizing credit card reward points for flights.

6. Bonus Tips for Beginners

Download a Map of Europe: Visualize the cluster. It roughly forms a shape starting from Spain, moving up to France, Belgium, Netherlands, Germany, dropping into Switzerland and Austria, and down into Italy. Group your travel to adjacent countries. Do not hop from Western Europe to Eastern Europe and back, as you will waste precious vacation time just sitting in trains or airports.

Do Not Obsessively Check Flight Prices Yet: Flight prices change dynamically. If you repeatedly search for the same route on your phone or laptop, browser cookies will signal your high intent to the airlines, and they will likely inflate the prices you see. Wait until you are absolutely ready to book.
